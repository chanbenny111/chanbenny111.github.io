Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 5, 2026

Short version: what you write is never saved, never uploaded and never seen by anyone. We don't collect any data at all.

What you write

The text you type and the optional "To:" line live only in your iPhone's memory while you write. They are erased the moment the burn starts; the picture of the page that plays the animation is kept in memory only until you leave the end screen (so you can make a video of the burn), then released. They are never written to disk, never included in backups, never sent over the network and never included in share cards. When the app goes to the background, the page is covered so your words don't appear in the app switcher.

The page also switches off autocorrect, predictive text, keyboard learning and Writing Tools, and the app does not allow third-party keyboards, so your words don't end up in a keyboard's dictionary or a keyboard developer's servers. After a burn, the text can't be brought back with shake-to-undo.

Photos you burn

You can burn a photo or screenshot instead of text. It is chosen with the system photo picker, which hands the app only the picture you pick — Let It Burn has no access to your photo library. The picture is kept only in memory, laid on the page for the burn and released with it (or when you leave the end screen). It is never saved, uploaded or included in share cards; the app keeps only a count of photo burns. If you allow saving to Photos, it is only so you can save your own video or share card from the share sheet.

Burn videos

If you tap "Share video" after a burn, the video is made on your iPhone from the picture of the page and the burn's timing (the loudness level in Scream mode and where you tapped). Your words are scrambled in the video unless you switch on "Show my real words" and confirm; photos are blurred unless you choose to show them. The soundtrack is generated by the app — your voice is never recorded. The video file is created in the app's temporary folder and deleted as soon as the share sheet closes. Nothing is uploaded by Let It Burn; where the video goes is up to you.

What stays on your device

To show your statistics, streak and widget, the app keeps a small record of each burn on your device only:

  • the date and time of the burn,
  • the ritual you chose and the prompt category, if you used one,
  • your mood before and after (if you chose one),
  • the word count, and whether it was an unsent letter or used Scream mode,
  • the rage score (a number from 0 to 100) if you screamed, and whether a photo was burned.

It also stores your settings (sound, haptics, reminder time and similar). This information is stored in the app's private container and a shared App Group container used by the widget. It never leaves your device. You can delete it at any time in Settings > Erase stats, or by deleting the app.

Microphone

Scream mode is optional. If you turn it on, a short explanation is shown, then iOS asks you for microphone access; the app reads only the current loudness level of the input to size the fire. Audio is never recorded, stored or transmitted. You can turn off access at any time in the iOS Settings app.

Purchases

Let It Burn Pro is a one-time In-App Purchase processed entirely by Apple. We never receive your payment details. The app checks your purchase status with Apple's StoreKit on your device.

Notifications

If you turn on the evening reminder, it is scheduled locally on your device. No push server is involved.

No tracking, no third parties

Let It Burn contains no analytics, no advertising, no crash-reporting SDKs and no third-party code. There are no accounts and no servers. We do not track you across apps or websites.

Children

Let It Burn does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and the date above.

This website and support email

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics, but GitHub may log visitors’ IP addresses for security, as described in GitHub’s privacy statement. If you email support, we receive your email address and what you write, and use them only to reply to you. The support inbox is a Gmail account, so Google stores those emails under its own privacy policy. You can ask us to delete them at any time.
